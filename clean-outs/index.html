Skip Bins for Clean-outs in Wodonga

Clean-outs in Wodonga: the short version

In short: A skip bin lets you clear a house, garage or shed at your own pace and have everything taken away in one go. Choose the size by how much you have, and keep banned items like asbestos, gas bottles, batteries and chemicals out.

Clean-outs are different from renovations. The rubbish is mostly furniture, boxes, clothes, garden waste and years of stored items, and the job often has a deadline: a settlement date, a moving truck or a final inspection. Below are the most common clean-outs around Wodonga and the Border region, with what to think about for each.

Deceased estate clean-outs

In short: Take your time, agree as a family or with the executor what's being kept before anything goes in the bin, and hire the skip once the sorting is done.

Clearing a loved one's home is hard, and it rarely goes to plan. If you're the executor or a family member arranging it, a few things help:

  • Make sure whoever is managing the estate is happy for items to be thrown out before you start.
  • Set aside papers, photos, keepsakes and anything valuable first. Look through drawers, pockets and boxes before they're thrown out.
  • Sort into keep, donate, sell and throw away. Charities and buyers often collect good furniture.
  • Older homes and sheds can contain fibro (which may contain asbestos), old chemicals, paint and gas bottles. These can't go in a general skip.
  • Book the skip once you know roughly how much is going, so you don't pay for days you don't use.

If family live away, mention it in the form. The operator can usually arrange delivery and collection without anyone needing to be there, as long as the bin's spot is clear.

Moving house or downsizing

In short: Clear out before you pack, not after. Book the skip for the week before moving day so you only move what you want to keep.

  • Start with the garage, shed, spare room and roof space, where the most unwanted things build up.
  • Sell or donate good furniture early, because it takes time to arrange.
  • When downsizing, measure your new home so you know what furniture will actually fit.
  • Leave space on the driveway for both the skip and the moving truck, or plan for the skip to be collected first.

End of lease clean-ups

In short: Remove everything that isn't staying with the property, including garden rubbish, before the final inspection.

Leftover furniture, rubbish or garden waste can hold up a final inspection. Check the condition report from when you moved in, then clear the house, garage, yard and any shed. If you're a landlord or agent clearing a place after tenants have left, describe what's there in the form so the operator can suggest a size. Most end of lease jobs suit a small to medium skip.

Garage and shed clean-outs

In short: Sort into keep, sell, recycle and throw away, and keep chemicals, gas bottles, batteries and fibro separate, because they can't go in the skip.

Garages and sheds collect old timber, broken furniture, tools, sporting gear and half-used tins of paint and garden chemicals. Scrap metal can often be recycled. Paint, oils, pool and garden chemicals, gas bottles and batteries need to go to a drop-off point for household problem waste instead. Old fibro sheeting may contain asbestos, so don't break it up or put it in a general skip.

Hoarder or big house clean-ups

In short: Work room by room, plan for more than one bin, and get specialist help if there are hazards like mould, sharps, vermin or chemicals.

A large or hoarded house can fill several skips. It's often easier to start with one big bin, see how far it goes, and book another. Wear gloves and sturdy shoes, keep walkways clear, and take breaks. If the home has mould, needles, animal waste, pests or chemicals, a specialist cleaning service may be needed before or alongside the skip. Be patient with the person whose home it is. Agreeing on what stays matters more than speed.

What can and can't go in the bin

In short: Furniture, household junk, timber, green waste and general clean-out rubbish are usually fine. Asbestos, gas bottles, batteries, tyres, chemicals and paint are not. Mattresses and e-waste often cost extra.

  • Usually accepted: furniture, boxes, clothes, toys, timber, carpet, garden waste and scrap metal.
  • Not accepted: asbestos and fibro, gas bottles, fire extinguishers, aerosols, batteries, paint, oils and chemicals.
  • Check first: mattresses, tyres, fridges, TVs and other e-waste.

Every operator has its own list. See what can go in a skip for more detail.

Choosing a bin size

In short: Go by volume, allow some spare room for bulky items, and remember heavy waste needs a smaller bin.

As a rough guide, 2 to 3 m³ suits a shed or garage, 4 m³ suits a small house clear-out or a bathroom, 6 m³ suits a kitchen's worth of rubbish or a big clean-up, and 8 m³ or more suits a whole house. A deceased estate or a family home with a big shed usually needs one of the bigger sizes. If you're unsure, choose "Not sure, help me choose" in the quote form and describe the job, and the operator can suggest a size. Frequently asked questions

Can I hire a skip bin for a deceased estate in Wodonga?

Yes. A skip is a common way to clear a deceased estate. Agree with the executor or family what's being kept before anything goes in, and tell the operator if no one will be at the property for delivery or collection.

What size skip do I need for a house clean-out?

As a rough guide, 2 to 3 m³ suits a shed or garage, 4 m³ suits a small house clear-out or a bathroom, 6 m³ suits a kitchen's worth of rubbish or a big clean-up, and 8 m³ or more suits a whole house. A deceased estate or a family home with a big shed usually needs one of the bigger sizes.

Can I put furniture and mattresses in a skip?

Furniture is usually fine. Many operators accept mattresses for an extra fee, and some don't take them. List them in the quote form so the operator can confirm.

What can't go in a skip bin during a clean-out?

Asbestos and fibro, gas bottles, fire extinguishers, aerosols, batteries, tyres, paint, oils and chemicals. Fridges, TVs and other e-waste may be refused or cost extra.

How long can I keep the skip?

Hire periods differ between operators. Most include a set number of days and charge for extra days. Tell the operator your dates when you request a quote.

Do I need to be home when the skip is delivered?

Usually not, as long as the spot for the bin is clear and the operator knows exactly where to put it. Add delivery notes in the form.

Are there other options for a small clean-out in Wodonga?

For a load or two, taking it to the Resource Recovery Centre on Kane Road yourself, using your two free tipping vouchers, may be enough. Council doesn't run a hard waste pick-up. Our rubbish removal guide compares the options.
