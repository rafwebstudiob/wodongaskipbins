What can and can't go in a skip

Waste type matters as much as size. Operators tip loads at licensed facilities, and each type of waste costs a different amount to dispose of. Wodonga Council's Resource Recovery Centre on Kane Road takes many recyclables free, but not asbestos.

Usually accepted

  • General household waste: furniture, toys, clothes, boxes, cupboard contents and general junk.
  • Green waste: gum branches, prunings, lawn clippings and hedge trimmings (often cheaper on its own, with no dirt or rubbish mixed in).
  • Renovation waste: timber, plasterboard, old cabinets, carpet, tiles and fittings.
  • Heavy or clean fill: soil, sand, concrete, bricks, rock and pavers (often needs its own bin and has a lower weight limit).
  • Scrap metal: old roofing iron, fencing wire, steel posts and outdoor furniture.

Not accepted, or extra charge

  • Asbestos and fibro: never in a normal skip (see below).
  • Tyres: usually banned or charged per tyre.
  • Car and household batteries: a fire risk in a skip.
  • Gas bottles and aerosols: can explode when compacted.
  • Paint, oils, chemicals and liquids: including pool chemicals, farm chemicals, sump oil and paint.
  • Mattresses: often accepted for an extra fee.
  • Food waste, medical waste and e-waste: check first. Many operators won't take TVs, computers or fluorescent tubes.

A note on asbestos

Many older Wodonga homes and sheds have fibro sheeting or asbestos-cement roofing. In Victoria, the state's asbestos guidance says a homeowner may remove up to 10 m² of non-friable (bonded) asbestos, but friable asbestos, and larger jobs, should go to a WorkSafe-licensed removalist. Asbestos waste can only go to a facility authorised to take it. See asbestos.vic.gov.au. If you suspect asbestos, say so on the quote form. Never hide it in a general skip.

Dropping things off yourself

Wodonga Council's Resource Recovery Centre is at 29 Kane Road. Council lists it as open Monday to Friday 9am to 5pm and weekends 10am to 4pm, closed Good Friday, Easter Sunday, Anzac Day, Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year's Day. Many recyclables are free to drop off, and general waste, green waste, timber, mattresses and tyres are accepted for a fee. It doesn't accept asbestos, cement sheeting or household chemicals. Call the centre on (02) 6022 9358 if you're unsure, and check the Wodonga Council website for fees.

Putting a skip on the street: Wodonga Council rules

Last updated: 24 October 2026

On your own property

Your own driveway, yard or a firm patch of ground is the easiest place for a skip. Council's permit is for skips on council land such as roads and nature strips, so a bin wholly on your property generally doesn't need it. On acreage, check the truck has a firm, dry track in and out.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

Wodonga Council requires a Permit to Place a Bulk Rubbish Container (Skip Bin) on Council Land under its Environment and Community Protection Local Law 2024, and placement must follow council's code of practice for waste bins on roadsides. Council's 2025–26 fee for the permit is $54.70, and public liability insurance may be required. Fees change each financial year, so confirm the current amount. On arterial roads managed by the state, the Department of Transport and Planning's rules for skips on roadsides also apply.

Before you book a skip on the street, check with Wodonga Council on (02) 6022 9300 or info@wodonga.vic.gov.au, or visit the council website. Mention it on the quote form too, because the operator may hold the permit or insurance.

Wherever the skip goes, keep it clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and footpaths, and don't fill it above the rim.
