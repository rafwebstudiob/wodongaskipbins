Skip bin permits in Wodonga: Wodonga Council rules

A skip on your own driveway usually doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or nature strip is different. Here's what Wodonga Council says and what to check before you book.

Last checked 24 October 2026

The short answer

On your own land, generally no. On a road or nature strip, yes: Wodonga Council requires a Permit to Place a Bulk Rubbish Container (Skip Bin) on Council Land. Its 2025–26 fee is $54.70.

On your own property

Your own driveway, yard or a firm patch of ground is the easiest place for a skip. Council's permit is for skips on council land such as roads and nature strips, so a bin wholly on your property generally doesn't need it. On acreage, check the truck has a firm, dry track in and out.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

Wodonga Council requires a Permit to Place a Bulk Rubbish Container (Skip Bin) on Council Land under its Environment and Community Protection Local Law 2024, and placement must follow council's code of practice for waste bins on roadsides. Council's 2025–26 fee for the permit is $54.70, and public liability insurance may be required. Fees change each financial year, so confirm the current amount. On arterial roads managed by the state, the Department of Transport and Planning's rules for skips on roadsides also apply.

Before you book a skip on the street, check with Wodonga Council on (02) 6022 9300 or info@wodonga.vic.gov.au, or visit the council website. Mention it on the quote form too, because the operator may hold the permit or insurance.

What to ask council before you book

  1. Does my spot need a Permit to Place a Bulk Rubbish Container on Council Land, and is the road managed by council or the state?
  2. What does it cost this financial year, and how long does assessment take?
  3. How long can the skip stay, and are there conditions such as lights, reflectors or a set distance from the kerb?
  4. Are there extra rules for a busy road, a bus stop or a heritage area?

Ask for the answers in writing or note the date and who you spoke to.

Placing the skip safely

Keep the skip clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and drains, and don't fill above the rim. Follow any conditions on the permit, such as reflectors or lights if the bin stays out overnight.

Where this information comes from

  • ABLIS: Permit to place a skip or large container on council land (Wodonga City Council)
  • Wodonga Council: Environment and Community Protection Local Law 2024 (PDF)
  • Department of Transport and Planning: placement of skips on roadsides

Council rules, hours and fees change. Check with the council or facility before you rely on them.
