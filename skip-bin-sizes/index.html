Skip bin sizes: which one do you need in Wodonga?

Skips are measured in cubic metres (m³). Here's how the common sizes compare, what jobs around Wodonga and nearby localities usually need, and how to avoid paying for space you won't use.

Last checked 24 October 2026

How skip sizes are measured

A skip's size is its volume in cubic metres. One cubic metre is a cube 1 m long, 1 m wide and 1 m high. To make that easier to picture, the table below compares each size with a standard 240-litre household wheelie bin (0.24 m³) and a 6×4 box trailer loaded level with its sides (roughly 1 m³). These are rough comparisons. Bin shapes vary between operators, so we don't quote exact dimensions.

Choosing the right size

Around Wodonga, a 2–3 m³ skip handles most shed, garage and small yard clean-ups, 4 m³ a bathroom, 6 m³ a kitchen or big garden job, and 8–12 m³ a house clear-out or a new build in Baranduda or Killara. Acreage clean-ups can need a larger bin, so describe the job in the form.

Pile everything up first if you can. A rough pile 2 m long, 1 m wide and 1 m high is about 2 m³.

Heavy waste needs a smaller bin

Soil, sand, concrete, bricks, tiles and rock are much heavier than household junk for the same volume. Operators usually limit heavy waste to smaller bins (often 2–4 m³) and may charge for extra weight or ask you not to fill past a line. If you're digging out a garden bed or breaking up a slab, choose "Soil, concrete or bricks" in the form so the quote is based on the right bin.

Some operators offer skip bags or larger hook-lift bins for commercial and acreage jobs. Heavy waste (soil, rock, concrete) usually needs a smaller bin because of weight limits.

Space and access for the truck

Skip trucks need room to reverse and lift. Check for low power lines, overhanging trees and soft ground after rain. A driveway is the easiest spot. If the only space is the road or nature strip, read the Wodonga skip permit guide first.

Not sure? Ask for help

If you can't decide, pick "Not sure, help me choose" in the quote form and describe the job in a sentence. The local operator who quotes you can suggest a size. If you think you're between two sizes, the bigger one is often cheaper than a second bin later, but check the price of both. See also what can and can't go in a skip.
